I use Python, C#, Arduino, and Unity. Python supports experiments, data processing, and PC logging; C# supports Windows programs and Unity games. Arduino reads sensors; Unity creates game scenes and behavior. The basic functions below lead to detailed guides at each level.

The same projects, revisions, and sources are covered at each level, which changes the **starting point and depth**. You do not need to advance through levels to see the material. Project names open the guide at this level.

## Reading revisions and source material

| Order | Read | Relationship to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Project purpose, inputs, and outputs** | Start here. Connect what is received, how it is processed, and what is produced. |
| 2 | **Revision documents at the same level** | Revisions 1 and 2 are development or experimental history; LEVEL 1 and 2 are reading levels. |
| 3 | **Folders, full comparisons, code, and papers** | Open these as needed to check evidence. Do not count earlier manuscripts from the same research as separate publications. |
| 4 | **Observed results and limitations** | Read together. Planning, implementation, execution, measurement, and publication are distinct states; one does not prove another. |

**When reading numbers:** Check conditions and units with accuracy, time, and sample counts. Do not combine manuscript values with later experimental results; distinguish rounded summaries from stored original values.
