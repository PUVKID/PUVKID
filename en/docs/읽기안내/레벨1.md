**For newcomers** — Start with **what was built and how it works step by step**, rather than technology names. Unfamiliar terms are explained with everyday examples and inputs and outputs.

I use Python, C#, Arduino, and Unity. Python supports experiments, data processing, and PC logging; C# supports Windows programs and Unity games. Arduino reads sensors; Unity is a tool for creating game scenes and behavior.

The basic project functions below lead to detailed guides at each level. The levels cover the same projects, revisions, and original sources; they differ in **starting point and depth**. You do not have to advance through levels to access material. Select a project name to open its guide at this level.

## Reading revisions and source material

| Order | Read | Relationship to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Project purpose, inputs, and outputs** | Start here. Connect what is received, how it is processed, and what is produced. |
| 2 | **Revision documents at the same level** | Revisions 1 and 2 are development or experimental history; LEVEL 1 and 2 are reading levels. |
| 3 | **Folders, full comparisons, code, and papers** | Open these as needed to check evidence. Do not count earlier manuscripts from the same research as separate publications. |
| 4 | **Observed results and limitations** | Read together. Planning, implementation, execution, measurement, and publication are distinct states; one does not prove another. |

**When reading numbers:** Check conditions and units alongside accuracy, time, and sample counts. Do not combine manuscript values with later experimental results; distinguish rounded summaries from stored original values.
